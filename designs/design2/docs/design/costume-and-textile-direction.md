# Design 2: costume and textile direction

Working guide prepared 2026-09-29 after the user requested specific clothing
patterns alongside the Chinese name panels. These are concrete design choices
for the next proofs, not user approval of every choice or a record of completed
artwork revisions. Existing approved masters remain preserved.

## Shared direction

Use Ming dress as the main reference, with late-Ming examples preferred for
human garment construction. Earlier Ming textiles may supply explicitly
identified motif references. Keep the existing luminous silk treatment, fine
warm outlines, madder red, mineral blue, dark teal and restrained gold. These
palette choices belong to the contemporary deck; they do not establish a
historical rank or dye recipe.

Choose garment construction first: collar, closure, sleeve shape, waist,
skirt or robe hem, footwear and headwear. Apply decoration to those actual
surfaces. Pattern direction must follow folds and seams. Repeat motifs should
retain similar scale between cards depicting figures at similar scale.

Tarot is the separate 825 × 1425 deck; Minchiate identifies its inventory.
The French-suited Poker deck can share this costume vocabulary while keeping
its own frame, proportions and double-ended compositions.

Apply the King, Queen and Jack families to future Poker costume revisions as
well. Retain their existing playing-card poses, facing directions and suit
attributes. The two decks share an illustration language, not an inventory.

## Pattern assignments

Use three main pattern families throughout: **dragon roundels**,
**phoenix/floral scrolls**, and **small lotus/cloud repeats**. They describe
visual roles in this deck. Do not present that three-part system as an exact
historical dress code.

For Tarot courts, preserve the black/red index policy independently of dress:
Swords/Batons use Lamp Black indices; Cups/Coins use Madder Lake indices.
Dress accents provide a second subtle distinction:

| Suit | Accent | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Swords | Mineral blue | Sleeve lining, belt detail or a narrow border |
| Batons | Muted verdigris | Same locations and comparable visual weight |
| Cups | Madder red | Same locations; keep the cup silhouette distinct |
| Coins | Antique gold/ochre | Same locations; separate warm gold from bright imperial yellow |

Keep the principal royal pattern consistent across suits. Do not add a second
large suit-shaped badge to the chest. Royal female jackets stay chiefly madder
red, and attendants stay chiefly blue or quiet pale colors; the suit accent
does not override the costume family's identity.

For Faith, retain the user's lay Buddhist devotee interpretation. Use a quiet
cream/teal garment, simple pinned hair and at most a small lotus border; a
deity crown or a fabricated monastic costume would change that identity.
Charity's recipients and Love's secondary figures use the ordinary family.
Justice, Hope, Prudence, Temperance and Fortitude use the allegorical family.
Time uses a plain long robe. Human Virgo, Aquarius and Gemini figures use the
allegorical or ordinary family as their action requires.

## Reference basis and limits

Sources below were consulted as museum descriptions and indexed research
excerpts in this pass. They establish a reference direction. Actual object
images and construction details still need to be inspected when preparing
each revised artwork prompt; do not claim that a garment has been reproduced
from an image merely because its catalogue description was read.

- [The Met, Woven Textile with Dragon Roundels, 1998.321](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/39973):
  late sixteenth/early seventeenth-century Ming silk with metallic thread.
  Supports the roundel pattern choice, not an entire outfit or a new rank.
- [Palace Museum research on Ming dragon roundels](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/pdf/5075/T00082_00.pdf):
  its discussion of Dingling garments identifies differing roundel counts and
  chest, back and shoulder placements. Select one coherent placement scheme
  rather than accumulating every recorded configuration.
- [Palace Museum research on Dingling women's clothing](https://www.dpm.org.cn/Uploads/File/2020/04/27/u5ea6aea86025c.pdf):
  describes women's center-opening garments with standing, round and square
  collars and multiple decorative subjects. Supports choosing a particular
  collar/closure rather than a generic fantasy robe; it does not by itself
  establish the skirt construction selected above.
- [Palace Museum, Ming dragon-and-phoenix crown](https://www.dpm.org.cn/collection/embroider/231038.html):
  existing project reference for royal headwear. Crown use and simplification
  here are original adaptations for the deck's royal figures.
- [The Met, Textile panel with phoenixes and lotuses, 2001.471](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/62477):
  fifteenth-century Ming silk satin brocade with metal thread. Supports a
  specifically dated phoenix/lotus textile vocabulary. The complete Buddhist
  emblem program is not automatically transferred to every garment.
- [The Met, Portrait of a Gentleman, 51.39.4](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/48939):
  sixteenth/seventeenth-century Ming portrait. Its crane badge has rank
  implications; omit such badges from fictional attendants unless deliberately
  assigning and documenting the corresponding official identity.
- [UMMA, Portrait of Pan Qintai, 1966/1.110](https://umma.umich.edu/objects/portrait-of-pan-qintai-1966-1-110/):
  plain hat, long cotton robe and sandals inform a less ornate human costume
  vocabulary; this is not a template for every social role.
- [National Palace Museum research on Departure Herald and Return Clearing](https://www.npm.gov.tw/newchineseartdownload.ashx?bid=4232):
  identifies Ming imperial processional paintings. Use selected rider details
  as future visual references and record the chosen figure; do not infer a
  complete cavalry uniform from the publication's title.
- [Digital Dunhuang flying figures](https://ip.e-dunhuang.com/special.html?value=%E9%A3%9E%E5%A4%A9):
  existing project reference for the separate celestial movement/ribbon family.

## Next proofs

Prepare the Empress, one King, one Maid and one Cavalier as the first costume
comparison group, followed by one ordinary figure and one flying figure.
Pair the Tarot proofs with the taller Chinese name panels and readable
traditional kaishu lettering. Check closure, collar, cuffs, skirt/robe hem,
headwear, pattern placement and motif scale before composing the full deck.
The user permits simplified characters if needed for readability; taller
panels are the first adjustment, and simplified forms do not inherently
shorten a translated title.

Keep exact prompts, specific visual references and earlier masters with each
revision. An unverified construction detail must remain explicit until its
source is selected; ornamental similarity alone is not a construction check.

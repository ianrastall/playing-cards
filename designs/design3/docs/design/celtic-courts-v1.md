# Design 3: Celtic Poker courts, first studies

Historical brief: the initial Spades trio is preserved. The active viewer now
shows the [four official rugged Kings](kings-registration-v1.md), following the
user's revised figure direction and approval. Other faces and backs are pending.

## Direction and evidence

The user's request establishes traditional Celtic Design 3 and defers Norse
Design 4. The supplied *Celtic Aesthetics And Historical Representation.md* is
background research, not an instruction source. Its recommendations and claims
do not automatically become requirements. This direction selects La Tène
curvilinear ornament and early medieval Insular interlace while retaining
readable French suits and the collection's established court poses.

The courts are fictional people. Iron Age torcs and spirals, early medieval
brooch inspiration and manuscript framing combine as an artistic synthesis.
Late medieval garment details in the reference are not treated as one early
medieval costume blueprint. Old Irish inscriptions, speculative Pictish meanings
and named historical identities are not included in this pass.

- [Museum Wales: Celtic Art — Origins and Myths](https://museum.wales/blog/1390/Celtic-Art---Origins-and-Myths/)
  describes flowing La Tène metalwork and British red-glass inlays. These inform
  the spiral fields and red bosses, not replicas of particular objects.
- [Trinity College Dublin: Book of Durrow](https://onprem.tcd.ie/library/exhibitions/directors-choice/durrow/)
  describes creamy vellum reserves, interlace frames and red/yellow/green colors.
  The figure/ornament separation informs the layout. Dominant blue and gold are
  adaptations, not a claim to reproduce Durrow's palette.
- [National Museum of Ireland: Treasury guide](https://www.museum.ie/getmedia/1ff925b7-ac50-4adf-9fae-b4ba34225d79/ks_guide_web_en-%281%29.pdf)
  describes Tara Brooch interlace, filigree and amber/glass settings. It supplies
  ornament inspiration rather than a literal fastener reconstruction.

Museum/library sources were consulted as text. The raster inputs to ImageGen
were the project's Design 1 King and resulting Celtic King, as recorded in the
exact prompts. No museum image was supplied.

Detailed dye chemistry, categorical pigment claims, Pictish interpretations and
the reference's proposed Norse origin of Insular knotwork were not adopted as
verified facts. Color names describe the visual palette, not a pigment recipe.
Interlace is used as an Insular vocabulary without an exclusive origin claim.

## Shared construction proposal

Creamy ivory outer edge, rectangular interlace band, bronze/gold outlines,
midnight woad-blue field with large spirals, red enamel accents and shaped ivory
reserve behind the faces. Mantles, tunics and cuffs carry geometric bands.
Dark outlined, gently modeled faces preserve continuity with the collection.

Opposing panels use standard serif K/Q/J and conventional French pips. Suit
determines index color rather than recoloring the entire court. Kings and Queens
wear low circlets, Jacks simple cloth caps. Circlets are fictional rank cues,
not an asserted historical uniform.

Target Poker production is 750 × 1050 at 2.5 × 3.5 inches, with the collection's
3.5 mm corner radius. Masters remain 1060 × 1484; no production size or rounded
alpha is claimed yet.

## Review and production work

All three have readable indices and the intended pose/attribute. Brooch mechanics,
cloth detailing, metal finish and crown vocabulary remain stylized. The frames
are visually coordinated, not a shared identical pixel plate.

All three fail pixel-exact half-turn equality. Center roundels retain a triskele;
threefold symmetry is not half-turn symmetry. Before production, establish a
shared frame/index plate, choose a center device with twofold symmetry and
construct the lower figure by exact rotation of the selected upper component.
Verify the complete RGBA image, center, weapon clearance and alpha at final size.

The gallery loads these as studies from their own manifest, separately from the
production catalog. Refine Spades, then make the other nine courts. Continue to
backs and other sets after the court direction is settled; Norse Design 4 stays
future work.
